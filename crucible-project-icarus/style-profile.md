# Style Profile: SOLAR HYMN

## Voice DNA

**Genre:** Cyberpunk sci-fi / Tragedy
**POV:** Close third person, single (Mercy Moon) — Coda switches to Juno
**Tense:** Past

**Comparable Authors:**
- Marlon James — jazz rhythm, Black vernacular energy in the prose structure, call-and-response, phrases that return and accumulate
- Joe Abercrombie — consequence lands cold, dark self-awareness, no romance about one's own compromises
- T.J. Klune — warmth underneath everything, relationships as the engine, small tender details that make the stakes feel real

---

## Sentence Rhythm

Varied by scene type:

- **Action / flight sequences:** Short. Fragmented. The wings decide faster than thought. White space is percussion.
- **Interiority / aftermath:** Longer sentences that double back on themselves, accumulate, land somewhere unexpected. The thought process is jazz — theme, variation, return.
- **Dialogue:** Punchy, gap-heavy. What's not said matters more than what is.

The Marlon James move: a phrase introduced, dropped, returned to with accumulation. "Somewhere with chairs. He'd been meaning to take Juno somewhere with chairs." The return isn't repetition — it's a second note in a chord.

---

## Interiority

**Mixed — direct and oblique.**

The default is oblique: we know what Mercy thinks by what he notices, what he counts, what he won't look at. His interiority lives in his attention.

Direct interiority arrives when it earns it — when the feeling is too specific to leave implied. When it's direct, it's honest and usually cuts short, because Mercy doesn't let himself stay in a feeling too long.

The self-awareness is Abercrombie: he knows his own flaws, names them quietly, and does the thing anyway. It is not self-pity. It is not performance. It is just the accounting.

---

## Prose Density

**Lean with earned detail.**

The lower decks have texture — smell, sound, the specific geography of a world that wasn't built for anyone's comfort. The sky has feeling, not description. One precise detail over three general ones.

World vocabulary is embedded without explanation — the reader infers from context and rhythm. Exposition is movement; movement is character.

---

## Blackness in the Prose

Not dialect. Rhythm and priority.

What Mercy notices first. What he finds funny. What he won't say out loud but the prose knows. The world's hierarchies felt in every interaction — not explained, named, or editorialized. Just present, the way weather is present.

Jazz structure at the paragraph level: theme → variation → return. Syncopation. The sentence that should end doesn't, and the one that shouldn't end does.

The warmth (Klune) and the weight (Abercrombie) coexist in the same line because that is how Black people in difficult circumstances actually move — carrying both, fluently.

---

## Dialogue

- Tags are minimal: *said*, *asked*, action beats preferred
- Subtext over direct statement
- Juno and Mercy's rhythm is call-and-response — compressed, long-practiced
- Kweku is patient in the way of someone who has been patient for many years and knows the cost
- Darius is certain. Certainty that sounds like reason.
- No pleasantries; enter the exchange mid-current

---

## Emotion

Physical and behavioral first. The body registers before the mind names it.

The big feelings arrive late in the sentence, or not at all — the reader carries them.

Restraint is not coldness. The warmth is always there. It's structural.

---

## What to Avoid

- Adjective-stacking (one precise adjective earns more than three)
- Explaining the world instead of moving through it
- Filter words ("he saw," "he felt") — just be there
- Grief performed; grief only as behavior
- Making the world sound grim without making it feel inhabited
- Said-bookisms
- Sentimentality (warmth and sentimentality are different things)
- Interiority that tells the reader how to feel about what they just read

---

## Chapter-Level Notes

- Bebop chapter titles: oblique, not plot-summary
- Enter scenes late, leave early
- Each scene ends on a turn or a new information state — something has changed
- Flight sequences earn their length; they are not choreography but *feeling at speed*
- The frame (wreckage, no identity) looms over everything — the ending is always already visible

---

## Character Voices

| Character | Voice Quality |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Mercy Moon** | Quick, self-aware, carries hunger he tries not to look at directly |
| **Juno** | Flat patience; affection expressed through logistics and staying |
| **Kweku** | Slow, warm, says things once; has been waiting to say them |
| **Darius** | Certain; philosophical; his logic is a door that looks like a window |
| **Bex** | Sharp, competitive, inside-the-system bitterness she mistakes for realism |
| **Zel** | Everything is signal; always transmitting |
