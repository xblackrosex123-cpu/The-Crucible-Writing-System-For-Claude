# Chapter 20 — Afterglow

**Movement:** Coda — Tempered Blade
**Beats:** 35 (Cost Revealed) · 36 (Blade's Purpose)
**Strand Focus:** Constellation / Forge
**POV:** Juno
**Estimated Words:** 3,500–4,500

---

## Purpose

The Coda is Juno's. She carries the full accounting — what was lost, what changed, what didn't. The world after Mercy Moon, seen from the ground. The novel ends on an implied sound: someone is going to fly. The sky is open in a way it wasn't.

---

## Scenes

### Scene 20.1 — The Accounting (Beat 35)
The Coda opens on Juno, not in crisis, in the quiet after.

She counts: who is gone — Bex, the movement organizer from Ch 16, the conscripted crew member from FP0 who didn't make it through; what changed — the monopoly cracked, Kweku's escape plan real and running, the lower decks with a route they didn't have; what didn't change — the solar city is still the solar city, the political fight isn't over, the people who loved Mercy are here and shorter by one.

The full price shown, without editorial. Juno doesn't grieve loudly. She counts.

**Turn:** The accounting is complete. She knows the shape of what it cost.

### Scene 20.2 — What Remains
Juno walks through the lower decks. People know her face now — she's part of the story even though she was always the ground. She doesn't know how to feel about that yet.

Kweku is in the workshop. The prototype wings aren't there — just the schematics, pinned where they've always been. He's working on something else. The project from Ch 7 that he never named — still running, still building. He hasn't stopped.

Zel's signal is still broadcasting: not the run, but the record. The archive of every impossible thing Mercy Moon did in the lower-decks sky. The solar city has it. You can't take it back.

The political fight isn't over. Darius is still there. But the architecture has shifted — what Mercy delivered opened a door, and doors, once opened, are harder to close than the people who opened them.

**Turn:** The world after. Changed and unchanged. Continuing.

### Scene 20.3 — The Blade's Purpose (Beat 36)
Kweku says one thing to Juno. Not a eulogy — a continuation. Something about what Mercy understood at the end that he didn't understand at the beginning. Something about what the wings were always for, and what the next pair will carry.

The novel closes on a sound: something that could be a ship warming up, a set of wings being tested, a frequency on Zel's signal that isn't old footage. Not named. Not shown. Implied.

Someone is going to fly.

The last image: the lower decks, looking up. The sky open in a way it wasn't. The looking-up meaning something different than it did on the first page.

**Turn:** The wound becomes a gift. The book ends.

## Tone
The quietest chapter in the book. No runs. No action. No crises. Just the after — honest, specific, not sentimentalized. Juno's voice should be practical and clear; she counts because counting is how she carries things. The final image should earn its stillness by arriving after everything that came before it. The implied sound at the end is not triumphant — it's continuous. The story keeps going. We just don't follow it anymore.
