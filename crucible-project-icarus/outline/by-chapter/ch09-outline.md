# Chapter 9 — System Blues

**Movement:** III — The Scattering
**Beats:** 19 (Institution Corrupted)
**Strand Focus:** Quest
**POV:** Mercy Moon
**Estimated Words:** 4,000–5,000

---

## Purpose

The full architecture of the cage becomes visible. Mercy sees the whole shape: the syndicate owns the government, the drug trade targets the organizing communities, and Mercy is the delivery mechanism for both. He is simultaneously the movement's symbol and its enemy. The question he can't yet answer: is there a run that goes the other way?

---

## Scenes

### Scene 9.1 — What Zel Found
Zel has been doing more than amplifying runs. They've been following the money. The evidence arrives in layers:

The syndicate's drug revenue funds specific political candidates, law enforcement contracts, infrastructure decisions. Darius isn't running crime. He's running the solar city's governance from beneath it. Every official channel is downstream of his operation.

Zel brings this to Mercy because Mercy is the only one with the access and the capability to do something with it. They believe in him. That makes it harder, not easier.

**Turn:** Mercy sees the first layer and knows the second is coming.

### Scene 9.2 — The Mirror
The second layer: the drugs Mercy is running aren't going to outside markets. They're flowing into the lower-decks communities that Zel's broadcaster network has been organizing. The addiction is targeted. The movement Mercy has become the symbol of is being chemically undermined.

And Mercy is the delivery system.

He has been, simultaneously, the face of the resistance and the mechanism of its suppression. The hunger for recognition made him useful to both. The empire designed this.

Mercy sits with this for a while. He doesn't leave the room.

**Turn:** The full architecture visible. He is a mechanism working as designed.

### Scene 9.3 — Zel's Hope
Zel is watching Mercy figure this out. They have hope — they came to him because they believe the information changes something, that Mercy will use it. Their faith in him is uncomfortable and real.

The question Mercy can't answer yet: *is there a run that goes the other way?* Is there something he can deliver that opens what this has been closing?

He doesn't answer Zel. He's not ready. But the question is in him now.

**Turn:** The question planted. The chapter ends on Mercy and the information and the question without an answer yet.

Information delivery chapter, but the information should feel like a physical weight arriving. Zel's scenes should have urgency — they're bringing this to the one person who might be able to do something. Mercy's processing scenes should be still and long. The question at the end is not hopeful — it's the first faint shape of a way forward in a chapter full of enclosure.
