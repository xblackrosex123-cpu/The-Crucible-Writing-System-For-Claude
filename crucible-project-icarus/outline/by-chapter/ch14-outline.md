# Chapter 14 — Reasonable Terms

**Movement:** IV — Brightest Burning
**Beats:** 26 (Dark Mirror's Offer)
**Strand Focus:** Fire / Quest
**POV:** Mercy Moon
**Estimated Words:** 4,500–5,500

---

## Purpose

The offer at its most dangerous — when Mercy is most vulnerable. Darius is right about almost everything. The chapter holds the gap where Mercy's answer should be. Neither yes nor no. The reader should feel, for the first time, that yes is genuinely possible.

---

## Scenes

### Scene 14.1 — Darius Arrives
Darius approaches Mercy directly, after Bex's death. The timing is deliberate and both know it. Darius doesn't pretend otherwise — it's one of the things that makes him comprehensible. He doesn't hide what he's doing. He shows his hand and lets the math do the work.

He comes to where Mercy is, in the lower decks, on Mercy's ground. That's also deliberate.

**Turn:** He's here. He has something to say and it isn't a threat.

### Scene 14.2 — The Offer
Darius has the wings' data. He's had people running Kweku's math since Ch 4, when Mercy first earned his attention. The tolerance curve ends. Not metaphorically — a timeline, a date, measurable consequences for the body. The wings are killing Mercy. Darius has a fix. Upper-city resources, the kind of medical and technical capacity the lower decks can't access.

The offer: *let me fix you. Fly for me — officially, with a future, with protection for the people around you. Stop burning for the myth.*

The price, left unstated but present in every word: Kweku — the wings' origin, the escape plan, the mentor who built them — becomes Darius's. And Mercy never flies free again. He flies on Darius's terms. The sky remains owned.

Darius is right about almost everything. The wings will kill Mercy. Kweku's escape plan won't work at scale. The lower decks have lost the political fight. The offer is not irrational. There is no lie in it.

**Turn:** The offer is real. The cost is real. Both are visible.

### Scene 14.3 — What Mercy Almost Says
He doesn't answer immediately. The chapter holds here — the gap where the answer should be.

He thinks about Bex, who had the legitimate version of exactly this. Who was sanctioned, sponsored, endorsed. Who died anyway coming toward him.

He thinks about what Darius was in the archive footage — young, in the free era, before the choice. He's looking at where that choice leads.

He says he'll think about it. Darius accepts this without pressure. He knows how to wait. He's been waiting since Ch 4.

**Turn:** Neither yes nor no. The clock is running.

---

## Chapter Turn
**Type:** Question raised
**The question:** Is there a version of yes that isn't surrender? The reader should know there isn't. Mercy should not know yet.

---

## Key Plants
- "He knows how to wait" plants FP3 — Darius's patience ends in Ch 16 when he moves without waiting
- "The wings will kill you" plants Ch 19 — Mercy chooses the fall knowing Darius was right
- "Bex had the legitimate version" plants Ch 15's refusal — the thing that closes the door
- The offer's exact terms plant Ch 15's "what he keeps" — the refusal is the inverse of everything offered here

---

## Tone
The most seductive chapter in the book. Darius should be at his most comprehensible and most generous. The offer should feel like care — because it is care, of a kind. The horror is not in what Darius is offering but in what it costs. The gap where Mercy's answer should be is the chapter's emotional center — hold it long, don't rush to the refusal.
