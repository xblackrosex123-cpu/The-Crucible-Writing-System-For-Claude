# Chapter 13 — Signal Loss

**Movement:** IV — Brightest Burning
**Beats:** 25 (Constellation's Sacrifice)
**Strand Focus:** Constellation
**POV:** Mercy Moon
**Estimated Words:** 4,000–5,000

---

## Purpose

The death that cannot be undone. Bex — the rival, the inside-the-system warning, the Mercy 1 recipient — dies because Mercy didn't come back. The mercy and the death in the same sentence. The grief carried quietly. The question he already knows the answer to.

---

## Scenes

### Scene 13.1 — The Absence
In the wake of Ch 12's peak, Mercy doesn't come back when he should. Not a deliberate choice — the myth has its own momentum now. The runs compound. The legend needs feeding. He's present in body and somewhere else in the story he's become.

People cover for him. They've been covering for him — the crew, Juno, the expanding constellation. He doesn't notice how long they've been doing it.

**Turn:** The ground has been holding things he doesn't see. This time, the gap his absence creates has a cost he can't cover.

### Scene 13.2 — Bex Crosses Over
Bex — the sanctioned racer, the one who stayed inside the system, the warning Mercy never took — goes looking for him. Crosses into lower-decks territory. Into the space where Mercy's world operates, the corridors that Bex's legitimate credentials don't fully cover.

Why: the specific reason belongs to drafting — but the shape is this: Bex chose Mercy over safety. They didn't have to go. They were safe in the sanctioned lanes. They went anyway.

They die in the crossing. Not in front of Mercy. Not witnessed. Not heroically — just the consequence of being in the wrong space at the wrong time because someone they cared about wasn't where he said he'd be.

**Turn:** The heroic choice was the crossing. The death is the result of it.

### Scene 13.3 — The After
Mercy finds out. The chapter holds here — just the information arriving and what it does to him.

The Mercy 1 arc closes: he showed mercy to Bex in Ch 3, a rival pilot he could have burned. Bex died coming toward him. The mercy and the death are in the same sentence. Whatever the mercy earned, it didn't prevent this.

He doesn't perform the grief. He carries it — quietly, the way he's learned to carry things across this book.

One question sits under everything: *if I'd come back, would Bex still be here?* He knows the answer. It's yes.

**Turn:** The constellation has a permanent gap. The Fire fed on the moment that created it.

## Chapter Turn
**Type:** Emotional beat
**The resonance:** The cost of the myth's momentum. The people who come toward him do so at their own risk, and he cannot protect them from the speed of his own becoming.

## Tone
The quietest chapter in Movement IV. No run. No action. Just information arriving and what a person does when they receive it. The chapter should resist dramatizing the grief — Mercy has learned not to perform, and the chapter should honor that by showing grief as weight carried rather than wound displayed. Bex's death should not be shown on the page — only its consequence.
